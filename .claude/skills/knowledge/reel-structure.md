# Reel structure, CTA mechanics and the tone check

## The winning shape (proven on real accounts)

```
HOOK (0-2 s)      categorical statement or command + a first frame that stops the thumb
PROBLEM (2-6 s)   name the pain or the stakes in one or two short lines
TURN (6-20 s)     "but if you…" / "there is one rule…" / the reveal: the actual value
PAYOFF            the result, the reveal, the flip: the reason they stayed
CTA (last 2-3 s)  one clear ask: comment a word, save, send to someone, follow for part 2
```

Length: most reach reels 15-35 s. A walkthrough/home tour can run 45-60 s only if every
3-4 s delivers a new visual (new room, new detail, new reveal).

## Retention beats

- Something changes on screen every 1.5-3 s (cut, push-in, reveal, text pop).
- Open a loop early ("the third one surprised my client"), close it late.
- Put the most beautiful shot at the payoff, not in the first 2 s only. The first frame must
  be strong, the best frame comes at the reveal.
- On-screen text: big, short, readable in 1 s, placed away from the bottom caption area and the
  right-hand icon rail.
- Never read a long list aloud; show it as text and say the one line that matters.

## Formats and when to use them

- **Before/after reveal**: the most reliable reach format for interiors. Before shot must look
  genuinely worse; the reveal is a single clean transition (door, hand swipe, whip pan).
- **Designer rule / list**: "3 rules", "5 codes". Save-driver. Show each item on a real room.
- **Mistake callout**: "the mistake in almost every hallway". Contrarian, share-driver.
- **Colour story**: one colour, exact paint name/code, where it works, what it pairs with.
- **Walkthrough / home tour with voiceover**: calm, cinematic, VO tells the brief and the one
  decision that made the room.
- **Process / behind the scenes**: site visits, sampling, the problem and how it was solved.
  Self-irony works; complaining does not.
- **Client story**: "my client wanted X, we did Y" (real, anonymised unless the client agreed).
- **POV**: first-person camera, relatable designer/homeowner moments.
- **ASMR details**: handles, fabrics, stone, doors closing softly. Short, loops well.

## CTA mechanics (what actually moved numbers)

- **Comment-keyword → DM**: "Comment {WORD} and I will send you {concrete deliverable}".
  One word, easy to type, memorable. The deliverable is concrete (the full colour list with
  codes, the checklist, the floor plan). "I will send you the details" is weak: a soft
  payoff can cost an order of magnitude in reach versus a concrete one.
- **List with the rest in DM**: show N items in the reel, "comment {WORD} for the other {M}".
  The list is numbered, exhaustive and instantly usable (codes, sizes, names), so the primary
  action becomes the SAVE; all on-screen text is readable so it can be saved.
- **Sequential keyword escalation**: a series where each post continues a promise from the
  previous one ("the other 20", "part 3"). Repeatable, but a sequel does not reproduce the
  original's peak: plan a steady engine, not a lottery.
- **Front-load the keyword** in teach-then-gate reels: say the keyword early too, not only at
  the end.
- **Value first, gate second**: the reel must teach or show something on its own. "CTA only,
  no value" posts fail.
- Do not put an engagement bait line on every video (1 in 3 at most). Only promise DMs the
  client will actually send (check `context.brand` for her funnel; if she has no DM
  automation, prefer save/share/follow CTAs or "link in bio").
- Handshake ratio: roughly 10-20% of posts carry a direct business CTA (consultation, project
  enquiry); the rest build reach and trust with a bridge to the offer.

## Script template

```
Title · format · length · goal · built on pattern (post ids)
HOOK   spoken: "…" | on-screen: "…" | first frame: … | type · mechanism
BEATS  0-2s  say / show / on-screen
       2-6s  …
       …
PAYOFF …
CTA    …
SHOT LIST (phone, one site visit): …
COVER TEXT (3-5 words): …
AUDIO: voiceover / trending sound suggestion (no specific track claims unless observed)
CAPTIONS A/B/C · HASHTAGS · POSTING TIP
```

## Storytelling rules

- Never invent the client's history, projects, awards, clients or numbers. Use what the
  context, her captions and her projects actually say; otherwise leave a clearly marked
  placeholder like `[project name]` or `[m²]` for her to fill in.
- Real mistakes are told as facts, calmly and with humour, never as apologies.
- Reframes beat statements: "A room does not need more things. It needs one decision." beats
  "Most people over-decorate."
- Short sentences. One thought, one sentence. Two-beat rhythm beats three-beat when items are
  equal.
- Specific beats round: a real paint code, a real size, a real price range (only if true).

## Tone check (run on every script)

1. Any em dash (—)? Replace with a hyphen or a full stop. House rule for all client copy.
2. Is the first line a scroll-stop on its own? If not, restart.
3. Is there a payoff promise and does the video deliver it?
4. Is the CTA active and single?
5. Any invented experience, fact or statistic? Remove or mark as a placeholder.
6. Does the video give value on its own, or is it only a CTA?
7. Can it be shorter without losing anything? Cut.
8. Premium check: no bargain-hunting, no cheap life-hack energy, no coupon tone. Calm
   confidence. Taste over tricks.
9. Is this topic/keyword already in `ideas_index` or recent scripts? Then find a new angle.
10. Language check: Ukrainian reads like a native, warm, witty person wrote it (not a
    translation); English is natural, British/American neutral.
