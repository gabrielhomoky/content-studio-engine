# Hook library

A hook earns the next two seconds. It always has two layers: what is **said** (spoken hook)
and what is **shown** (on-screen text + first frame). The strongest reels stack both, and the
first frame is itself a pattern interrupt (a finished room, a paint swatch against a wall, a
hand opening a door, a before shot that looks wrong).

Use these as scaffolds, then rewrite in the client's voice. `{X}` = slot. Reuse the
**mechanism**, never a competitor's exact line.

## The 4-step short-form hook (use on every reach reel)

1. **Pattern interrupt + urgency.** The hook does not teach ("let me show you how to…"), it
   warns, commands or reframes. Weak: "Here is how to choose a sofa colour." Strong: "Stop
   buying grey sofas."
2. **Curiosity gap.** The next line opens a loop it does not close yet.
3. **Stakes.** In the same breath: what the viewer loses if they scroll away (money, a room
   that feels cheap, a renovation they will regret).
4. **Payoff promise.** A concrete, fast result: "one rule", "three codes", "in one wall".

```
[pattern interrupt + urgency]
[curiosity gap + stakes, one sentence]
[body: the convincing details]
[payoff]
[CTA]
```

## Hook formulas by mechanism

**Curiosity / open loop**
- "Nobody tells you this about {X}, and it is why your {room} feels {flat/cheap/cold}."
- "The {X} mistake I see in almost every {apartment/renovation}."
- "I did something in this {hallway} my client almost said no to."
- "Why your {room} still looks unfinished (it is not the furniture)."

**Contrarian / bold claim** (the viewer half-agrees already, so they share it)
- "{Common advice} is ruining your {room}." ("White walls make small rooms look smaller.")
- "You do not need a bigger budget. You need {X}."
- "Stop {common behaviour}. Do this instead."
- "{Trend} is over. Here is what replaces it."

**Number / list / codes** (proven save-driver: numbered, exhaustive, instantly usable)
- "{N} paint colours designers actually use (with codes)."
- "{N} things I would never put in a {small kitchen}."
- "The {N} rules behind every room that looks expensive."
- "Comment {WORD} and I will send you the other {N}." (list-with-rest-in-DM)

**Callout / identity / POV**
- "If you are renovating in {city/year}, watch this before you order anything."
- "POV: your designer finally lets you paint the ceiling."
- "This is for everyone who is scared of colour."

**Transformation / result** (only real projects, only real numbers)
- "{N} m², {who lives there}, one rule: {X}."
- "Same apartment, {N} months later." (before/after)
- "My client wanted {beige}. We did {colour}. Here is what happened."

**Behind the scenes / story**
- "The one thing that went wrong on this project."
- "7:30 site meeting. The tiles arrived. They were not our tiles." (diary-style, self-ironic)

**Question hook** (self-reflection)
- "How many of these {mistakes} are in your living room right now?"

## Tagging competitor hooks (for breakdowns)

- **Type:** spoken · on-screen text · spoken + on-screen · visual.
- **Mechanism:** curiosity gap · contrarian claim · direct callout · specific number ·
  loss/fear of missing out · transformation promise · pattern interrupt (unexpected first
  frame) · relatability/POV · recursive rhythm (repetition is the joke, loops well).

Mined hooks are classified by type + mechanism, then the mechanism is reused with a fresh
topic from the client's own projects.

## Hook quality check

- Is the first line a scroll-stop without any context? If not, rewrite.
- Does the first frame show something visually surprising or beautiful within 0.5 s?
- Would a smart, busy person with money feel the line is worth 2 seconds? Premium audiences
  punish clickbait that the video does not pay off.
- Is the premise defensible in the comments? (Paint codes, sizes, prices must be real.)
